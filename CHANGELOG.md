# Changelog

What changed in each release of Arteria. The release workflow copies a version's section into its GitHub release notes, so write for people running the addon.

## Unreleased

- **Better device recognition with Fingerbank (optional, off by default).** Arteria works fully without it. Add your own free key from [api.fingerbank.org](https://api.fingerbank.org) in the page's new **Settings** (or with `arteria.sh fingerbank`, or menu option *f*), and Arteria asks Fingerbank what each device is from the DHCP options it asks for, which tell an iPhone from an Echo or a Fire TV Stick even with a private MAC address and no name. Adding a key shares the option list and the DHCP vendor class with Fingerbank, and after *Recognise now* the names of the services each device announces and its UPnP server string: never a MAC address, name or IP address on your network. Settings explains how to get a key and asks you to keep it to yourself (the free key allows 300 lookups an hour), and shows only its last 4 characters. Answers are kept per kind of device (15 identical bulbs are one lookup), and Fingerbank now comes right after the icon you chose and before the device's name. To do this, dnsmasq runs a small script of Arteria's before the router's own lease script, also on renewals, and a new fingerprint is looked up at once, page open or not. *Recognise now* (or `arteria.sh fingerbank now`) asks the devices on your network what they are, over mDNS (Bonjour) and UPnP as phones find printers and speakers, and looks each one up again with the services it announces, which tell Fingerbank far more than a DHCP fingerprint alone (that often only says *Android* or *Linux*). Nothing is disconnected; it needs Entware's `socat` (`opkg install socat`), and without it only does the lookups. Pressing it again asks every device afresh, Fingerbank included. A new device is asked the same way automatically, a minute after it joins, page open or not. A Fingerbank answer that only says "a smart device" (Generic IoT) no longer outranks a name that says what the device is. The model a device announces (`MacBookPro18,1`, *Echo 4th Gen*, a printer's or a NAS's model) decides its kind right after the icon you chose, before Fingerbank; Details and the Device report say so. `arteria.sh fingerbank compare` shows, for each device that answered, Fingerbank's answer with its DHCP fingerprint alone, with its services, with its maker and with its whole MAC. `fingerbank off` and `uninstall` put dnsmasq back as it was. Details and the Device report say when Fingerbank recognised a device.
- **Air purifiers and humidifiers, smart switches and door sensors** each get their own drawing and icon: a white tower with its water tank, a light switch on a piece of wall, and a sensor with its magnet across a door's edge.
- **Tapo, Kasa and Govee devices recognised by the names they give themselves**: Tapo and Kasa use their model number (`L630` and `KL130` are bulbs, `P110` a plug, `C200` a camera, `S505` a switch, `H200` a hub, `RV30` a vacuum), and Govee its maker and model (`ihoment_H6159_…` is a light strip, `H7121` a purifier, `H5075` a thermometer). A *Philips Air* device is an air purifier.
- **Labels keep off other devices**: when every spot next to a device would cover another device's drawing, its label moves further out, with a longer line to its device.
- **A nickname the router saved by itself isn't used as a name**: the router's client editor fills in what it shows, so saving just an icon can store "dhcpcd-6 8 2 Linux…" or the maker's name as the nickname. The device's own name is shown instead (the icon you chose still counts).
- **Anything cut short with "…" shows in full when you point at it**: device names on the map and in Flow, a node's name, a maker, a path.
- **Three new kinds of device**, each with its own drawing and icon: **Raspberry Pi** (a green board with its chip, pins and ports), **streaming stick** (Fire TV Stick, Chromecast, Roku Stick, with its remote) and **smart speaker** (a fabric cylinder with a light ring: Echo, Echo Studio, HomePod, Nest Audio). Apple TV, Fire TV Cube and Shield stay streaming boxes; Sonos, Bose and the like stay speakers.
- **More devices recognised by name**: Ring cameras (`minicam`, `floodlightcam`) and the Ring Chime, MyQ garage openers, thermostats (`tstat`, Lennox iComfort, Honeywell), Xiaomi and Redmi tablets (`Xiaomi-Pad-7`), Sony and Samsung TVs by model number (`KD-43X85J`), HP Envy printers in host names like `hpenvy-7640`, EV chargers (ChargePoint, Wallbox, Easee), `SP111` smart plugs, WLED light strips, subwoofers, and *kamera* for a camera. A name like *Alexa's iPhone* is a phone, not a smart speaker.
- **`nodes test` shows whether the page can read each node.** Besides key login, it runs one round of the script the page runs on each node, and reports how many devices it read and how long that took, or why it failed (with addresses and user names taken out). A node that is too slow for the page says so. `debug` no longer lists nodes as down while the page is closed, and shows each node's last SSH message.
- **`nodekey` with SSH password login turned off** (*Allow Password Login: No*): it no longer tries a password the nodes won't take. The key goes into the router's own Authorized Keys, which AiMesh copies to the nodes at its next settings sync, and `nodekey` says how to start one: save a setting (the Wireless page works), wait a few minutes, then run `nodes test`. The same when a node turns down passwords on its own.

## 1.0.0

The first release: a live, animated map of how traffic flows between the internet, your router, its AiMesh nodes and every device.

- **Map**: an isometric view of your network. Cables are pipes and Wi-Fi links are arcs; each packet is traffic in motion, and packets grow and bunch up as throughput rises while moving at the same calm speed. Blue is download (toward the device), orange is upload (toward the internet), violet is the AiMesh backhaul. Devices behind a node stand in its zone. On a busy network the smallest devices are grouped into a *+N more* tray.
- **Flow**: a Sankey "river" from the internet (and local traffic) through the router, each band, port and node, to every device. Ribbon width follows throughput, with Download and Upload views. Rows keep a fixed order, so nothing jumps around while you watch.
- **Tap any device** to trace its path: everything else dims, and the readout shows its download and upload, its share of the total, signal, link rate and the path its traffic takes. **Details** shows its IP and MAC address, host name, maker, system, Wi-Fi standard and channel width (or port and speed), connection time, IPv6 and DHCP lease.
- **27 kinds of device**, each with its own drawing and icon: desktop PCs, Mac desktops, laptops, phones, tablets, watches, TVs, streaming boxes, speakers, soundbars, game consoles, printers, NAS, cameras, doorbells, smart locks, thermostats, lights, smart-home hubs, air conditioners, robot vacuums, appliances, smart plugs, sensors and more. The icon you choose in the router's client list always wins; otherwise the device's name, what the router detected, and its maker decide.
- **AiMesh nodes and the router drawn as what they are**: plug-in extenders, towers, cubes, pucks, gaming routers and classic routers, by model.
- **The *+N more* tray opens a list**: tap it to see the grouped devices, busiest first, and tap any of them to trace it.
- **Private Wi-Fi addresses** are marked as such, and a device whose private address changed is recognised by its host name when that's unambiguous, so it keeps its name and icon.
- **Expanded view**: a button opens a bigger view that fills the window (or the screen), with a stand for every device on a typical home network, a third line on each label, Flow rows with address, signal and link rate, and the readout's traffic and details together.
- A **Device report** (at the bottom of the page) shows how each device was recognised, without names or addresses, for issues about a device drawn as the wrong thing.
- **AiMesh nodes, per device.** The router reads each node's own clients over a single SSH session per node, with a key only the router holds (`arteria.sh nodekey` sets it up, shows a node's fingerprint before trusting it, and keeps the key on the nodes across AiMesh syncs). Without the key, the map still shows each node's backhaul total.
- **Only while the page is open.** Sampling starts when you open the page and stops about 20 seconds after you close it or switch to another tab. Nothing runs in the background, there are no cron jobs, and samples stay in RAM.
- **Mb/s or MB/s**: a switch at the top shows every rate in megabits or megabytes per second, and the page remembers your choice.
- The *Whole network* readout splits each total into internet and local traffic (between your devices, or to the router itself), so it's clear why it's higher than the Internet figures.
- Notices for a weak or offline backhaul and for nodes that can't be read.
- A terminal menu, ready for amtm, with `amtmupdate`, `update dev [branch]` for trying unreleased changes, and a `debug` report with no names or addresses.
